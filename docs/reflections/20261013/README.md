## Before teaching

After teaching on the first day, I added the 'Why is R important?' 
and 'Why is `ggplot2` important?', as well as a demo to the schedule.
It felt like fixing my own teaching. I think it is interesting
that have missed this important part of a lesson. That I missed this
is easy to explain: my private life feels rushed and too-little-time too.

## During teaching

I did the two better-late-than-never sections at the start,
then I let the people that want to work in their breakout rooms,
while others would see the demo. The majority stayed.
Then I did the demo. This went smoothly, except for copy-pasting into
the Zoom chat: it sometimes took ten seconds to load. I used the Slack
as a backup for pasting quickly.

I asked some learners if the demo was useful and if I should do that
demo on day 1. One stated that the demo was useful, but day 1 would be
too early. Another stated that the demo was useful and that it should
have been on day 1.

- [x] do the demo on day 1

I think I should do the demo on day 1, so I added this to the schedule
to remind me.

Such opposite opinions reflect the diversity of the group well: it had
everything from complete beginners to not-really-needing-to-be-there
experts.

After the demo, I went through all the rooms. I noticed I had created too few
rooms, so some learners cramped together in a room first, and later moved
to fill up a vacant room.

At around 10:30 around three fourth of the learners were already asking
questions about how to communicate clearly with a plot. At around 11:30,
I understood from the last learner that he/she had a plot,
so my mission has been successfull.
